About Me

I am a 3rd-year Biostatistics Ph.D. student at the University of Nebraska Medical Center, specializing in the development and application of joint models, causal inference, and dynamic prediction for complex clinical data. My research focuses on leveraging advanced statistical methods to solve challenges in clinical trial design and analysis. I have 1 year of experience consulting and collaborating with multidisciplinary teams, managing and analyzing clinical data, and presenting results through tables, listings, and figures using data visualization in R and SAS for non-statistician audiences.

Research Experience

Graduate Research Assistant

Institution: University of Nebraska Medical Center, Omaha, NE

Period: August 2023 – Present

Dissertation: Joint analysis for multivariate biomarkers with a change point anchored at interval-censored event time, with applications to Huntington's Disease (HD)

Topic 1: Two-Phase Joint Model

  • Developed a two-phase joint model to handle change points of biomarkers and interval-censored data
  • Used I-spline to approximate cumulative hazard and Fisher-scoring algorithm for MLE
  • Applied model to PREDICT-HD data, demonstrating that cognitive biomarkers (sydigtot, stroopwo) predict HD onset and that HD onset accelerates biomarkers' decline

Topic 2: Causal Mediation Analysis and Dynamic Prediction

  • Developed causal mediation analysis using g-formula to compute natural direct and indirect effects (NDE/NIE)
  • Applied to HD data showing causal effects of CAP (disease burden score) on biomarkers and survival through two pathways
  • Derived joint distribution for dynamic prediction of event time and biomarker trajectories
